About this tool

What it is

A calculator that turns a patient's current benzodiazepine regimen into a withdrawal schedule based on the Ashton Manual. It runs entirely inside your browser. Nothing you type is transmitted anywhere, stored on any server, or seen by anyone else.

What it is not

It is not validated, not approved, and not a clinical decision-making authority. It has not been reviewed by any regulatory body. Every schedule it produces requires independent review by a qualified prescriber before use.

How it works

A schedule has up to three parts. An on-ramp — labelled Generated — bridges from the patient's actual dose onto a published Ashton schedule, substituting to diazepam one dose at a time starting with the night dose, and reducing by no more than one tenth at each step. From there the dose sequence follows a published schedule, labelled Published and cited by schedule and stage number, reproduced from the manual.

A citation is not an endorsement. Where a step reads Schedule 1, Stage 9 — Ashton 2002, that identifies where the dose sequence came from so a prescriber can check it against the source. It does not mean Professor Ashton recommended this schedule for this patient. The entry point, the path, and the step durations are chosen by this tool and by the prescriber. Where a duration falls outside the range Ashton published, the step is marked duration modified and the published range is shown.

The rules this tool follows

Every generated step follows the rules below. They are stated here rather than left implicit in the software, so that a prescriber can check the tool's reasoning against the source instead of trusting the output.

Scope

Alprazolam, lorazepam, clonazepam and diazepam only, using US tablet strengths. Seven published schedules are in scope (Ashton Schedules 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 7 and 8) giving 157 possible join points. Schedules involving nitrazepam, temazepam, oxazepam, chlordiazepoxide or zopiclone are excluded. The tool declines above 120mg diazepam-equivalent, the highest published starting dose.

Known defect carried from the source

Schedule 1, Stage 9 prints a daily total of 80mg where its component doses sum to 70mg. This is an error in the original manual. The component doses — what the patient actually takes — are used, and the discrepancy is preserved rather than silently corrected.

Schedule codes

Each schedule gets a code that regenerates it exactly. The code stores the inputs, not the output, and is checksummed so that a mistyped character is rejected rather than silently producing a different schedule. Codes are locked to the tool version that made them. A code encodes patient dosing and dates: treat it as protected health information.
